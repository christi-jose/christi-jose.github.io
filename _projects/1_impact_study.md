## Overview
**Duration:** May 2024 – Present  
**Location:** Toronto, Ontario  
**Organization:** Southern Ontario Centre for Atmospheric Aerosol Research (SOCAAR), University of Toronto  
**Funding:** Health Effects Institute (HEI)

The Improved Assessment and Characterization of Traffic-Related Particulate Emissions (IMPACT) study investigates the contribution of vehicle emissions to urban air quality.

## Key Contributions

### Field Campaign Leadership
- Deployed Universita Personal Air Samplers (UPAS) and passive Ogawa badges at 40 sites across Toronto
- Measured PM₂.₅, PM₁₀, NO₂, and NOₓ concentrations across diverse urban microenvironments
- Integrated high-resolution AirSENCE sensor data for temporal analysis

### Mobile Monitoring
- Conducted mobile sampling using electric vehicle (Tesla Bolt)
- Deployed TSI DustTrak, Testo DiscMini, and MicroAeth for high-resolution measurement
- Generated spatial pollutant maps and identified localized emission hotspots

### Sample Analysis & Modeling
- Performed X-ray fluorescence (XRF) analysis for elemental composition
- Conducted gravimetric analysis for PM mass quantification
- Applied Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to identify emission patterns
- Generated GIS-based predictor variables using Python and QGIS
- Developed Land Use Regression (LUR) models for urban pollutant distributions

## Technical Skills
- Advanced instrumentation: UPAS, Ogawa, SMPS, Aethalometer
- Geospatial analysis: QGIS, Python (geopandas, matplotlib)
- Statistical modeling: PCA, LUR regression
- Environmental fieldwork and mobile monitoring
